> This is the running specification: it describes what the game does today, in the present tense.
> Any change to observable behaviour updates this file in the same change. Design the code does

## 1. Overview

**Pitch.** Correspondence chess in a quiet club room: one move a day against a rated opponent,
with the platform's sandboxed script as the only referee — and a practice board against *hal* that
needs no sign-in at all.

|---|---|

## 2. Vision and design pillars

1. **One file is the referee.** `server.js` is the only authority on legality, results, colours and
   Elo, and the client loads *that same file* for move highlighting and replay stepping — and runs it
   as the offline opponent. Rules in: identical legal-move dots online and offline; the replay is a
   re-run of the move list. Rules out: any client-side rules copy, any client-reported result, any
   trust in the payload's identity.
2. **One move a day.** The game is paced like a letter, not a blitz clock. Each move hands the
   opponent a fresh 24 h; the bar under the clock drains over the day and turns red in the last hour.
   Rules in: a menu that sorts games by "your move" and deadline, a warning cue at the last hour.
   Rules out: increments, live timers, anything that rewards being online at the same time.
3. **A quiet club room.** Walnut, ivory, brass and paper; a scoresheet that fills as you play;
   sounds that are wood on wood and a small bell. Rules in: a single brass accent. Rules out:
   confetti, neon, score pop-ups, music.
4. **Every seat is rated — hal's too.** The server AI carries the same persistent Elo and record as
   any member and is offered after 30 s of unmatched queueing so a player always has a rated game to
   play. Rules in: hal games count. Rules out: a separate "casual" ladder online (the only unrated
   board is the offline practice game).
5. **The board takes any hand.** Pointer, touch, keyboard and gamepad all play the same board; pieces
   are vectors so both colours render correctly everywhere. Rules out: drag-and-drop as the only
   input, glyph pieces, hover-only affordances.

## 3. Player experience

**Target player.** Someone who likes chess and has fifteen minutes here and there — the commuter
who answers three games over coffee — plus anyone who wants a board to poke at with no account.

**First 60 seconds.** Signed out, the club screen reads "Take a seat" with one primary button,
**Play**, under "Practice against hal right now — no sign-in needed." Pressing it opens the board in
a random colour. The side panel says "Your move" or "hal to move"; if hal is white it replies within
0.45–0.95 s. Clicking or tapping one of your pieces rings it in brass and drops a dot on every legal
destination (a ring on captures); clicking a dot plays the move and the scoresheet gains a SAN entry.
The game teaches by affordance rather than text: the dots *are* the rules, the last move stays
tinted, the king in check glows red and "Check" shows by your seat, the promotion dialog appears only
when a pawn reaches the last rank. There is no tutorial screen; the toasts ("Illegal move.", "hal
declines the draw.") are the rest of the learning surface.

**Session shape.** Offline: a full game against a greedy opponent, usually ending in mate or
resignation within 20–40 moves. Online: sign in → the club lists your games sorted "your move"
first, then by nearest deadline → open one, play one move, read the table talk, back to the club →
repeat, or press Play to queue for a new opponent. Finished games land in "Recent games" and open in
the replay viewer.

**Emotional beat.** The satisfying *knock* of a piece landing on wood and the quiet certainty that
the referee cannot be argued with — then the small bell when a check lands.

## 4. Core loop and rules contract

All rules live in `server.js`; function names below are from that file unless stated.

**Board and state.** `newGameState()` returns `{ board, turn, castling{K,Q,k,q}, epSquare,
halfmoveClock, fullmove, moves[], positionCounts{}, status }`. `board` is a 64-character string,
index = rank×8 + file, `a1` = 0, `PNBRQK` white, `pnbrqk` black, `.` empty. The opening position is
counted once in `positionCounts` (`positionKey()` = board + turn + castling rights + ep square).

**Legal actions** (`legalMovesFrom(g, from)` → `allLegalMoves(g)`): pseudo-moves from
`pseudoMovesFrom` filtered by `inCheck` on the resulting board (`applyMoveToBoard`); promotions are
expanded into four moves (q, r, b, n). Castling requires king on its home square (file e) and rook
rights intact, empty squares between, and the king neither in check nor passing through or landing
on an attacked square (`isAttacked`). En passant is offered only onto `g.epSquare` set by the
previous double push. Rights are removed when a king moves or a rook moves from or is captured on its
corner (`applyMoveToBoard`).

**Resolution order for one move** (`makeMove(g, {from,to,promo}, now)`):
1. Parse squares (`Malformed square.`), validate promo (`Invalid promotion piece.`).
2. Find the request among the legal moves; a promotion without a piece is refused with
   `Promotion piece required (q, r, b or n).`, anything else with `Illegal move.`
3. Apply; compute check and mate on the post-move board; build SAN against the pre-move board
   (`sanFor`: file, then rank, then full-square disambiguation; `O-O`/`O-O-O`; `=Q`; `+`/`#`).
4. Update clocks: `halfmoveClock` resets on pawn moves and captures; `fullmove` after black.
5. Append `{from,to,promo,san,at}` to `moves`, count the new position.
6. Terminal checks, in this order: checkmate → stalemate → threefold repetition (count ≥ 3) →
   fifty-move rule (`halfmoveClock ≥ 100`) → insufficient material (`insufficientMaterial`: K v K,
   K+minor v K, K+B v K+B with same-coloured bishops). Draws are automatic, never claimed.

**Server-side command handling** (`game.onPlayerMessage`): sender identity comes from the host, not
the payload. `sync` returns the view to the sender only. On an active game: `move` (turn checked:
`Not your turn.`) clears any draw offer and sets `deadline = now + 24 h`; `resign` ends the game for
the opponent; `offer-draw` records `drawOfferBy`, a second offer from the other side is an agreement,
hal declines instantly and records nothing; `accept-draw` / `decline-draw` require a live offer from
the other colour. Unknown commands are refused. Only what the script returns in `broadcast` is
relayed.

**Move clock** (`game.onTick`, `tickRateHz: 1`): when `now ≥ deadline` on an active game, the side
to move loses on time (`timeout`) — unless no move has ever been played, in which case the game is a
draw (`timeout-no-moves`). Both outcomes are rated.

**Practice wins**: every game against hal that the human wins (by any reason) adds one to the human's `practiceWins` and posts the new total to the `practice-wins` board through the returned `scores`.

**Colours** (`game.createSession`): first meeting of a pair is decided by the host-supplied
`ctx.random < 0.5`; every later game between the same two alternates, remembered per player in
`lastColorVs` (LRU of 200 opponents). Sessions against hal are random every time. A session needs
exactly two distinct players. The offline practice session goes through the same `createSession`.

**hal** (`greedyPick(g, seed)`): one-ply material greed — score a move by the captured piece's value
(p1 n3 b3 r5 q9) + 1 for en passant + (promotion value − 1); ties broken by a small LCG seeded from
the host's per-invocation random, so a given invocation is deterministic. `aiReply` plays hal's move
inside the same invocation as the human's, and opens immediately when hal is white.

**Rating** (`finishGame` → `eloAfter`): Elo with K = 32, start 1200, rounded to integers, applied
to both player documents with wins/losses/draws counters and a `recentGames` summary (last 30).
Expected score `E = 1 / (1 + 10^((b − a)/400))`; new rating `a + 32·(S − E)`.
Worked example — white 1300, black 1200, draw: `E_white = 1/(1+10^(−0.25)) = 0.640`;
white → `1300 + 32·(0.5 − 0.640) = 1295.5 → 1296`; black → `1200 + 32·(0.5 − 0.360) = 1204.5 → 1204`.
Two 1200s: the winner becomes 1216, the loser 1184.

**Terminal states.** `result.kind ∈ {white, black, draw}`, `result.reason ∈ {checkmate, stalemate,
threefold-repetition, fifty-move-rule, insufficient-material, resignation, agreement, timeout,
timeout-no-moves}`. There are no tie-breaks beyond a draw being a draw.

**Undo and hints.** No undo, online or offline. The only hint is the legal-destination dots for the
selected piece; there is no engine advice.

**Offline practice differences.** The client runs `server.js` as a local authority
(`starhermit:script` `authority`, players `you` and `hal` with `ai: true`), so legality, hal, draw
refusal and game end are the script's own. It is unrated (a local session awards nothing), has no
clock, chat, voice or replay, and ends when the player leaves the table.

## 5. Modes and progression

| Mode | Entry | What differs |
|---|---|---|
| Offline practice vs hal | Signed out: **Play**. Signed in: **Practice vs hal** | Local authority; unrated; no clock; no chat/voice/replay; ← Club returns to the club |
| Rated matchmaking | Club → **Play** | `matchmaking.enqueue`; nearest-Elo pairing; ticket polled every 3 s; after 30 s a **Play against hal** button appears (the 30 s belong to the ticket and survive a restart, stored per player) |
| Rated game vs hal | Matchmaking → **Play against hal** | `sessions.practice()`; server-side hal; 24 h clock applies; no table talk or voice |
| Friend invite | Club → **Invite a friend** | Dialog listing platform friends; accepting creates the session |
| Share link | Club → **Share invite link** | Copies the platform invite link (`inviteLink()`) to the clipboard |
| Rejoin | Club → "My games" | Opens the session; rows show "your move"/"their move" and time left |
| Replay | Club → "Recent games", or **View replay** on the game-over dialog | Read-only board stepped from the archived move list |

Difficulty comes from the opponent, not the mode: hal is a fixed one-ply greedy player, humans are
matched by nearest Elo. There are no unlocks, no daily puzzles and no seeded content. Progression is
the "My rating" panel (rating, W/L/D) and the friends' ratings table.

| Play move | Click / tap a dotted square | Focus it, Enter/Space | South | Move sent; selection cleared |
| Reselect / deselect | Another own piece / any other square | Same | Same | New candidates, or selection cleared |
| Promotion | Button in the dialog | Tab + Enter | — | Queen, Rook, Knight, Bishop; closing the dialog cancels |
| Draw / Resign | Side-panel buttons | Tab + Enter | — | Confirm dialog |
| Accept / decline draw | Banner buttons | Same | — | Only shown to the side that did not offer |

| Voice | **Enable voice**, **Mute**/**Unmute**, **Leave voice** | Same | — | Microphone prompt from the runtime; human opponents online only |

Declared actions (`control.*`, rebindable in the runtime): `up`, `down`, `left`, `right`, `row_start`,
`row_end`, `select`, with gamepad defaults (`engine.gamepad.*`). Action ids allow lowercase letters,
digits and underscores only.

**Board focus.** The first arrow / select press on a game screen gives the board the keyboard and
shows a brass focus square (on your own back rank's e-file). While it has it, the board owns the
arrow, Home/End and select keys; Tab or Escape returns them to the widgets, and any dialog takes them.

**Input locking.** Board presses are ignored unless it is my turn on an active game. While a dialog
is open the board takes no input. After game over Resign and Offer draw are disabled; Offer draw is
also disabled while my own offer is pending.

**Feedback for every input.** Selection ring; destination dots; last-move tint on both squares;
red glow on a king in check; a toast for any rejected command (`illegal` knock); connection lines
("Connecting…", "reconnecting…", "connection closed"); sounds per §9.

## 7. Screens and UI flow

`shell.ts` holds one current screen; `show()` disposes the previous one (closing its socket, chat
channel and voice room).

```
club(guest) ──Play──▶ table(practice) ──← Club──▶ club
club ──row/Play/Accept──▶ table(online) ──← Club──▶ club
club ──Recent games──▶ replay ──← Club──▶ club
table ──View replay──▶ replay
player changes (sign in / out) ──▶ club
```

**Club.** Signed out: "Take a seat", **Play**, "or sign in to the club", **Sign in with StarHermit**
(the runtime's sign-in; in browsers it leaves for the platform's sign-in page and returns signed in).
Signed in, two columns (stacked when narrow): Play (rated **Play**, **Practice vs hal**, the search
state with **Cancel** and, after 30 s, **Play against hal**), "My games" (refreshed every 30 s),
"Invitations" (refreshed every 10 s; accept/decline, **Invite a friend**, **Share invite link**);
side: "My rating" (Elo and W/L/D), "Friends", "Recent games".

**Table.** `splitLayout()`: board left with the opponent's seat above and mine below, side panel
right; on a tall window the panel goes below the board. Side panel: ← Club, Offer draw, Resign; the
turn line; online the clock with a 24 h bar (red under an hour); the draw banner; the scoresheet
(scrolling); table talk and voice (online, human opponents). Dialogs: promotion, confirm ("Resign this
game?", "Offer a draw to {name}?"), game over (result, reason, the new rating and practice-wins rank
online; **View replay**, **Back to the club**, **Look at the board**).

**Replay.** The board at the chosen ply with both seats, the result line ("Alice won — by
checkmate"), the stepping buttons with a "12 / 37" counter and the clickable scoresheet.

**Must never be cut off.** The board is sized to fit the window (the smaller of the height left after
the seats and the width left after the panel), the panel scrolls inside its own areas, and toasts
stack bottom-centre within the window. Layout honours the runtime's safe area and UI scale.

## 9. Audio direction

**Mix philosophy.** Dry, close-miked and small: wood on wood, a fingertip on a table, one brass bell.
No music and no ambience — the room is silent so that a piece landing is an event. Every clip is
loudness-normalised to −20 LUFS and under three seconds. `select` and `illegal` play on the runtime's
interface bus, the rest on the effects bus; volumes are the runtime's Options › Audio. Clips load in
the background at start (`loadClip`); a clip that fails to load or play is silent, never an error.
Move cues are derived from SAN (`sfxForMove`): `=` promote, `O-O` castle, `x` capture, otherwise
move, plus the bell when the SAN ends in `+` or `#`.

**SFX event table** (source of `sfx/manifest.txt`):

| Event id | File | Sound | Usage context |
|---|---|---|---|
| `select` | `piece-select.opus` | A wooden piece lifted off the board, one light dry tap with a faint slide | Own piece selected |
| `move` | `piece-move.opus` | A wooden piece set down gently, one soft solid knock with warm resonance | Any quiet move landing, either side; forward step in the replay viewer |
| `capture` | `piece-capture.opus` | A piece knocking another off the board, a firm double clack with a small tumble | Capture landing (SAN contains `x`) |
| `castle` | `piece-castle.opus` | Two pieces set down in quick succession, two soft knocks a beat apart | `O-O` / `O-O-O` |

| `promote` | `promote-chime.opus` | A short ascending glockenspiel triplet ending on a sustained note | Promotion landing (SAN contains `=`) |
| `illegal` | `illegal-knock.opus` | A dull muted knuckle knock on a tabletop, two damped taps | A refused command |
| `matchFound` | `match-found.opus` | A hotel desk bell struck once in a quiet carpeted room | Matchmaking ticket became `matched` |

Voice chat (`starhermit:platform` `voice`, open mic) is a separate opt-in channel, off for every new

## 10. Localization

Nine locales ship for the whole UI: **en-US, en-GB, es-419, es-ES, de-DE, fr-FR, fr-CA, pt-BR, it-IT**.

- **Tables.** `strings.js` holds every player-facing string keyed by its English text: one row per key
  with es-419, de-DE, fr-FR, pt-BR and it-IT, plus en-GB, es-ES and fr-CA tables that list only their
  differences (they fall back to en-US, es-419 and fr-FR). `native/i18n.ts` loads it and gives the code
  `T()`. Chess notation (SAN, `O-O`, square names) and the names StarHermit, hal and Elo are
  locale-invariant. Some rows are kept from the earlier browser client and are no longer used.
- **Language.** The runtime's language preference (`preferences().language`, set in its Options menu
  or by the platform account), mapped to the nearest shipped locale (`es-MX → es-419`, `pt-PT → pt-BR`,
  unknown → en-US). A change re-renders the next frame in the new language; toasts already on screen
  keep theirs.

## 11. Accessibility

- **Keyboard-only path.** Club → Play (Tab + Enter) → arrows move the board's focus square, Enter or
  Space plays it → Tab to Offer draw / Resign → the dialog → game-over buttons. Replay: ← → Home End.
- **Gamepad.** The same path with the D-pad/stick, South and the shoulders; widgets follow the
  runtime's gamepad navigation.
- **Colour is never the only signal.** Check has the "Check" text as well as the glow; urgent time
  turns red *and* reads "0h 42m left"; captures use a ring shape, not just a colour.
- **Scale.** Everything is laid out in units of the runtime's UI scale.

## 12. StarHermit integration

there is no hand-written platform layer. Signed out it makes no platform requests.

## 13. Technical architecture

**Modules.** `main.ts` wires the screens into `shell.ts`'s router, routes touch/pointer presses that
no widget took to the screen (a pointer press within 600 ms of a touch is dropped), and routes actions
and keys. Screens (`club.ts`, `table.ts`, `replay.ts`) implement `update()` (lay out widgets and draw,
once a frame) plus optional `press`, `action`, `key`, `dispose`. `board.ts` draws the board and maps
points to squares. `rules.ts` loads `server.js` with `loadClassic` (its Node static-server tail stays
dormant) and exposes `replayMoves`.

**One path for online and offline.** Online, `connectGame` sends the same commands the script
expects (`sync`, `move`, `resign`, `offer-draw`, `accept-draw`, `decline-draw`). Offline the local
`authority` runs `server.js` with the same messages; hal's replies are held back 0.45–0.95 s and later
messages queue behind them so the order holds.

**Determinism and replay.** The session document *is* the move list: the client rebuilds full rules
state by replaying the moves through `makeMove` (`replayMoves`). The script receives time and
randomness only from `ctx.now` and `ctx.random`; hal's tie-breaks come from an LCG seeded by that
random.

**Persistence.** `starhermit:storage` `matchmaking.<userId>` (ticket id and queued-at). Volume,
graphics, bindings and language are the runtime's. Server-side: per-player document (Elo, record,
`lastColorVs` LRU 200, `recentGames` 30) and the session document with its `summary`.

**Runtimes and publishing.** One package serves both runtimes. The repository root is the package;
`index.html` (the web loader) and `starhermit-files.json` (the file index) are generated by
`STARHERMIT=<starhermit.exe> npm run package`, which copies the files git would commit to a temporary
folder (so local `node_modules/` or `.starhermit/` never reach the index) and runs
`starhermit package --web` there. Run it after every change and commit both files; `npm run
test:native` fails while the index is stale. Publishing is pushing to GitHub and pinning the commit
in the game's Manage menu: browsers then load the loader at `<uid>.starhermit.com`, which pulls engine
1.1.0 from wiki.starhermit.com and runs the TypeScript in a worker; the Windows client installs the
same files and plays them natively. The platform admits at most 9,998 paths and 50 MiB (the package
is about 1.2 MB).

**Performance budgets.** About 2,000 lines of TypeScript; piece images are rasterized once per
square size and cached; widgets are immediate-mode. Server invocations stay far under the 250 ms
CPU / 32 MB sandbox limits: perft(3) of the full generator runs in the tests in well under a second.

## 14. Testing and acceptance criteria

`npm test` = `npm run test:rules`, then `npm run test:native`. Type-check with
`npx --package typescript tsc -p .`.

**`tests/rules.mjs`** loads `server.js` as a plain script and checks: perft(1..3) = 20 / 400 / 8902;
castling, en passant and promotion play and score as `O-O`, `exd6`, `b8=Q+`; SAN disambiguation by
file, rank and square; threefold repetition counts the opening position; fool's mate `Qh4#`, a
stalemate, K+N v K insufficient material; a game hal ends still publishes `eloUpdates` and both
player documents; resignation, draw agreement, `timeout` and `timeout-no-moves` end games with the
right winner; out-of-turn, illegal and malformed commands are refused without recording anything;
colours alternate for a pair; `tickRateHz` and `replays` are declared; a one-player session is refused.

**`tests/native.mjs`**: the committed `starhermit-files.json` matches every file git would commit
(else "run npm run package"); then, with `STARHERMIT` pointing at the engine's `starhermit.exe`,
`starhermit check .` and every `tests/native/*.play` script signed out and offscreen —
`practice-flow.play` (practice opens, the draw offer hal declines, back to the club) and
`practice-game.play` (a move by keyboard with either colour, resignation through its dialog, the
game-over dialog, Look at the board).

**QA bar (checkable).**
- `tsc` is clean; `starhermit check .` passes; the web index is fresh.
- The offline path works signed out on both runtimes with no platform.
- A new player gets guidance from the UI itself: legal-move dots, last-move tint, the check glow and
  text, the "Practice against hal" hint and result reasons in words.

## 16. Known limitations

- Opponent chat arrives on the platform's ~5 s poll.
- No screen-reader output: the board is drawn, not DOM, and the engine exposes no accessibility tree.
- The cover tile bakes an English title into the image, and server-side refusal messages outside the
  known list are shown as sent.
- hal is a one-ply material-greedy player: it never plans, so it is a beginner's opponent.
- Offline practice cannot be replayed or resumed; leaving the table ends it.
- A launch cannot open a given session directly; invitations are accepted from the club.
- In browsers sounds start only after the first press (autoplay policy), and the web runtime logs a
  warning while it fetches the clips at start.
- The play scripts run signed out, so the online path (socket, matchmaking, invites, chat, voice,
  replays) is type-checked and covered by `rules.mjs` at the script level only. Play scripts press
  widgets by label, so board clicks were checked by hand in a browser, and promotion is not scripted.
